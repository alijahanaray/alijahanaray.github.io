NVForward Annual Executive Summary — Year 2

October 2025 – September 2026 · Prepared October 28, 2026 · Ali Jahanaray

NVForward enrolled 474 apprentices in Year 2 and graduated 165 of 193, a graduation rate of 86.1% against a 65% target. Graduates finished in a median of 15.7 months and 96% hold a Nevada teaching license. Verified placement and retention figures are not yet available and are the main gap in this report.

The two cohorts that finished during the year performed well: PPP 8.0 completed at 97.6% and PPP 9.0 / RSA 4.0 at 89.4%. The two largest intakes, PPP 10.0 / RSA 6.0 (209 apprentices) and PPP 11.0 / RSA 7.0 (85), started in Spring and Summer 2026 and are still in progress, which is why the unadjusted graduation figure across all apprentices is 34.8%.

Time to completion is stable and short. Across the full program history, 743 graduates from eleven cohorts finished in a median of 10.9 to 15.7 months, and only 8 have ever taken more than 24 months. Year 2 cohorts sit at the longer end of that band: PPP 8.0 at 15.7 months and PPP 9.0 at 14.8, against 10.9 for PPP 5.0 and 11.6 for PPP 7.0.

Workforce entry and retention

170 of the 177 Year 2 graduates hold a Nevada teaching license, 96.0%. Licensure is consistent across groups: 93.7% of Hispanic graduates, 97.1% of White graduates, 95.5% of Black graduates, and 100% of Asian and multiracial graduates. Across all apprentices enrolled during the year, 410 hold a license, 81.8%.

Placement as teacher of record in a high-need or hard-to-staff school is not reported for Year 2, because district employment records were not available in time. Hawkins measures (b), (e), and (g) remain open, and licensure is offered here as the closest available proxy for workforce entry, not as a substitute.

Verified retention is also pending. CREA reports that alumni retention cannot yet be calculated, as the relevant graduates have not completed a full year as teachers of record.

Guides on Demand was made available to 100% of alumni, against a 75% target. Self-reported intent runs high but is not a retention measure: of the 43 graduates surveyed at exit, 95% expect to be teaching in Nevada in one year and 93% in three.

Women are 428 of enrolled apprentices (85.4%) and 152 of 177 graduates (85.9%). Average age at program start is 35.7 and average cumulative GPA is 3.54, confirming that the program serves working adults rather than traditional-age undergraduates. 51 Year 2 completers, 31%, are bilingual or multilingual (Hawkins measure (c)). Bilingual placement, measure (d), is not yet reported.

Program quality and graduate experience

Satisfaction cleared its Year 2 target. 88.9% of respondents across four professional development modules reported satisfaction with RTI content, against a 75% target. Of the 43 exit-survey graduates, 88% were satisfied with the program overall, 86% would choose it again, and 93% felt prepared to teach. Satisfaction with coaching and concierge services was 86.4%, against a 73% target.

CREA's pre- and post-survey comparison against a traditional-pathway group found the change in self-efficacy favored NVForward by 0.42 points (SE = 0.26, p = .119), and by 0.47 points among those who took both surveys. Neither difference is statistically significant, which CREA attributes to survey attrition and a small comparison group. NVForward apprentices also earned an estimated 0.13 higher GPA in major-specific upper-division coursework.

Three themes recur across the exit survey and the eight alumni interviews. Tuition coverage and paid employment are why working adults enroll and finish; six of eight alumni said financial aid was decisive. Classroom readiness is uneven, with alumni who had worked in their own school feeling ready while others met curriculum, IEP, and evaluation demands the coursework had not covered. Support thins after graduation and licensure is a point of confusion, with response times and problem-solving rating lowest in the survey at 56–63%.

Data notes and open items

The program master file independently confirms the core Year 2 figures: 177 graduates, the same race and sex distribution, and 170 licensed graduates. The student-level data files are the source for every figure in this report except Year 2 enrollment and graduation rate, which follow the Hawkins status chart. Where earlier documents differ, the differences are listed below so they can be reconciled in one pass.

  • Enrollment (474) and graduation rate (86.1%, 165 of 193) follow the Hawkins status chart. The student-level extract shows 501 and 85.1% (177 of 208); the enrollment breakdown, licensure, and diversity figures still use the extract's 177 graduates.
  • The CREA annual evaluation report also records 474, consistent with the status chart.
  • The master file carries 30 records flagged as possible duplicates and 6 repeated NSHE IDs, 11 of them among Year 2 apprentices.

Still outstanding: district employment records for Hawkins (b), (e), and (g); the bilingual placement indicator for (d); and verified alumni retention.
